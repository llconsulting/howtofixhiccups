# louislynn Website Master Playbook

Version 1.0 | October 12, 2026 | Owner: Mike Buczynski

## Purpose

Create distinctive, credible, beautifully executed websites that help the right visitors take action. Reduce repeated corrections by making business facts, creative direction, approved decisions, and verification requirements explicit.

The ambition is the craft of a leading New York or Los Angeles creative studio, adapted to the actual business and customer. Every client deserves exceptional execution. Every client does not need the same aesthetic.

This document replaces the original anti-AI checklist as the master operating reference. It retains the useful house preferences while adding strategy, art direction, copy, UX, accessibility, SEO, engineering, and delivery standards.

This is a working instruction set for an LLM-assisted team. It is not a claim that any checklist can guarantee creativity, rankings, accessibility conformance, or conversions.

## 1. How to use this document

1. Put this file in the project repository or persistent project knowledge.
2. Reference it from the coding tool's supported project-instruction mechanism. Verify that the tool actually loads it; merely uploading a file does not guarantee future use.
3. Complete the Project Record in Appendix A using existing materials before asking Mike for missing information.
4. Use the appropriate kickoff, revision, or review prompt in Appendix B.
5. Keep approved project decisions separate from universal preferences.
6. Update the Project Record after meaningful approvals or corrections.

For a small site, this single file plus a completed Project Record is sufficient. For a larger project, split the record into `project-brief.md`, `creative-direction.md`, `page-map.md`, `decision-log.md`, and `qa-report.md`. Do not maintain conflicting duplicate instructions.

### Working modes

| Mode | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| New build | Discovery, direction, representative design, implementation, verification, preview |
| Redesign | New-build work plus existing-site inventory, content preservation, URL mapping, and migration checks |
| Targeted revision | Smallest coherent change, relevant regression checks, updated decision log |
| Audit | Inspect and report evidence; change files only when requested |

Do not restart discovery or redesign an approved site for a small edit.

## 2. Decision priority and autonomy

Within the project, follow the latest explicit client/Mike direction, verified business requirements, approved decisions, and these defaults. Respect the execution environment's governing instructions and access controls.

Resolve routine implementation details independently. Ask only when missing information materially changes strategy, scope, factual claims, cost, access, or a difficult-to-reverse decision. Batch necessary questions. State reasonable reversible assumptions and continue.

Use two meaningful review points for a new direction:

1. A concrete representative design before extending that direction through the entire site.
2. The completed, tested preview before production launch.

If a direction has already been approved, continue under that authorization. Do not repeatedly request approval for the same decision. Mike's default production workflow is preview first, explicit approval before pushing live. Do not treat approval of a design concept as production-launch approval.

### Three classes of rules

**Launch blockers:** fabricated proof; broken primary conversion; exposed secrets; critical accessibility barriers; accidental production noindex; materially false business information; destructive migration errors; missing required production approval.

**House defaults:** no eyebrows, no decorative section numbering, no em dashes, no generic framework aesthetic, no generic CTA language, no automatic breadcrumb strips. Follow unless a project-specific direction explicitly changes them.

**Contextual guidance:** font count, image placement, section variation, motion, supporting CTAs, and content length. Use judgment grounded in the visitor's needs and approved creative direction.

Do not give a punctuation preference the same severity as a broken form. Do not use contextual flexibility as permission to silently ignore Mike's taste.

## 3. Discovery and factual integrity

Before proposing the site, establish:

- Primary audience, buying situation, desired customer, and poor-fit inquiries.
- Main offer, supporting offers, geography, capacity, and conversion goal.
- Alternatives customers consider and reasons this business wins or loses.
- Real customer questions, objections, decision criteria, and language.
- Verified proof: projects, outcomes, reviews, credentials, process details, and experience.
- Existing brand assets, photos, usage rights, transcripts, analytics, URLs, and integrations.
- Platform constraints, editing needs, ownership, budget, and launch conditions.

Use available source material first. Ask for the smallest set of missing inputs that changes the work.

### Maintain a claims register

| Claim or asset | Source | Status | Allowed use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A business fact | Client document, URL, or confirmation | Verified / needs confirmation / outdated | Exact supported scope |
| Testimonial | Original review or approved quote | Verified and permission checked | Approved attribution and excerpt |
| Image | Client file or licensed source | Rights and provenance checked | Intended pages and crops |

Never invent results, awards, customer names, review counts, years in business, qualifications, warranties, pricing, response times, service locations, or client relationships.

If proof is missing, use supported process detail, actual work, or a clearer explanation. Keep unresolved facts in the internal report. Omit unsupported public claims. Do not publish placeholders or describe internal research gaps in customer-facing copy.

Do not promise a quote by Friday, a free consultation, same-day service, or a response within 24 hours unless the business has confirmed it.

## 4. Strategy and page architecture

Write a short positioning statement identifying the customer, offer, practical benefit, and supported reason to choose this business.

Build a page map before producing full layouts. Each important page needs:

- A defined audience or visitor situation.
- A distinct purpose and relevant search intent.
- A primary conversion goal.
- Required information and proof.
- Objections it needs to resolve.
- Useful internal links and its place in navigation.
- A reason to exist separately from neighboring pages.

Combine overlapping pages when they serve the same need and have little distinct substance. Create separate pages when the visitor task or offer warrants them. Do not build extra pages merely to increase page count.

### Section-purpose test

Before designing a section, state its job in one sentence: explain the offer, demonstrate work, establish credibility, compare options, resolve an objection, qualify fit, or enable action.

Remove a section when it repeats another section's job without adding value. Do not impose the same sequence of hero, logos, three services, process, testimonials, FAQs, and CTA on every page.

A possible customer journey is recognition, understanding, evidence, reassurance, and action. Adapt the order to how this customer buys. This is not a mandatory section template.

## 5. Creative direction before code

Produce a compact art-direction brief before expanding the site:

1. **Organizing idea:** a concrete creative concept tied to the business.
2. **Desired impression:** three specific qualities and the visual decisions that express them.
3. **References:** a small curated set with notes on what to learn from each.
4. **Typography:** heading and body roles, scale, weights, line height, and reading width.
5. **Composition:** grid, alignment, density, whitespace, image scale, and rhythm.
6. **Palette:** intentional roles and accessible foreground/background pairings.
7. **Photography and graphics:** subject matter, cropping, treatment, and available sources.
8. **Signature treatment:** one memorable business-relevant element.
9. **Motion:** what moves, why, and reduced-motion behavior.
10. **Mobile interpretation:** deliberate changes in hierarchy, crop, navigation, and interaction.

Words such as premium, modern, bold, or clean are insufficient without observable choices.

Example: “Architectural typography, restrained warm neutrals, large project photography, precise material captions, and an unusually clear project-planning path” is usable direction. It is an illustrative concept, not the default louislynn template.

### Reference discipline

- Use visual references to establish quality and specific design principles.
- State whether the reference informs typography, cropping, spacing, sequencing, or interaction.
- Do not copy a competitor's branding, copy, illustrations, or distinctive composition wholesale.
- Do not claim to have inspected references that were inaccessible.
- Use provided screenshots when live references cannot be inspected.
- If references are unavailable, propose a clear direction and label it as a proposal.

Recommend one direction. Produce additional alternatives only when a meaningful strategic choice remains unresolved; avoid spending time on three superficial color variations.

### Representative design

Prove the direction with the homepage opening and one substantial interior section, using real copy and assets where available. Show desktop and mobile. For a complex site, include a representative service or project page as well.

The sample must demonstrate the design language beyond a beautiful hero. Once approved, extend it consistently.

## 6. Visual craft standards

### Hierarchy and composition

- Establish one dominant focal point in each major view.
- Use scale and whitespace to make the intended reading order obvious.
- Give the site one defining visual idea and a limited number of supporting moments.
- Vary composition with a consistent grid, spacing system, typography, and interaction language.
- Create quiet sections that allow stronger moments to stand out.
- Avoid both repetitive card stacks and random layout changes.
- Review the entire page silhouette as well as individual sections.
- Make the header, footer, contact page, and secondary pages feel equally finished.

### Typography

- Default to one or two font families; use additional faces only for a documented reason.
- Define roles instead of selecting sizes and weights ad hoc.
- Use fluid sizing with sensible limits. Inspect actual line breaks at multiple widths.
- Default body copy to a comfortably readable size and roughly 45–75 characters per line where appropriate; assess the actual typeface and audience.
- Keep substantial body text left-aligned. Center short text only when the composition benefits.
- Avoid tiny navigation, pale captions, overly tight leading, and excessive all-caps text.
- Use licensed fonts and efficient loading. Check fallback behavior and layout shifts.
- Never make essential copy an image solely to preserve a visual treatment.

### Brand tokens

Define shared tokens for colors, type roles, spacing, content widths, borders, radii, shadows, and motion. Use them across the project. Do not introduce arbitrary values to patch every local issue.

Brand tokens must be intentionally selected. Renaming framework defaults does not create an identity. If the existing brand fails accessibility or lacks web-ready rules, propose compatible adjustments.

### House anti-patterns

- No decorative eyebrow labels, short accent dashes above headings, or automatic 01/02 section numbers.
- No decorative pills or tag chips. Functional filters and category controls may use suitable shapes.
- No generic three-icon feature rows, emoji icons, or icon-in-tinted-square repetition.
- No purple-to-blue gradient defaults, gradient headline text, glowing orbs, or generic gradient blobs.
- No default glassmorphism or soft-shadow card aesthetic.
- No automatic centered hero with vague headline, one-line subhead, and two generic buttons.
- No cards around everything. Use cards for discrete items that benefit from grouping or selection.
- No checkmark lists as the default presentation of every benefit.
- No invented star rows, client logos, metrics, or stock-photo testimonials.
- No fade-in on every block, hover lift on every card, or auto-rotating testimonial carousel.
- No automatic breadcrumb strip. If deep information architecture creates a genuine navigation need, explain it before departing from the house preference.

### Images and asset direction

- Prefer authentic work, people, places, materials, and products when they are relevant and visually usable.
- Do not force a weak photo above the fold. Design around the strongest available assets and message.
- Do not generate people for client-site imagery under the default house rules.
- Never present generated projects, facilities, products, or results as documentary evidence.
- Non-documentary illustration or abstract imagery can be appropriate when the creative brief supports it and provenance is clear.
- Stock assets must be licensed, relevant, and selected with care. Never misrepresent stock people or locations as the client's own.
- Record sources and permissions; visual inspection cannot prove authenticity.
- Crop deliberately for each major breakpoint. Inspect faces, text, materials, and focal points.
- Supply responsive sizes and efficient formats with appropriate fallbacks.
- Reserve image dimensions to prevent layout movement.
- Provide a short prioritized shot list when missing photography materially limits quality.

## 7. Copy standards

Write in American English with a clear, specific, human voice appropriate to the client. Use the operator's language as source material, then edit for clarity and persuasive structure.

### Every important page must answer

1. What can this business do for me?
2. Is this relevant to my situation and location?
3. Why should I believe it?
4. What should I know before deciding?
5. What happens when I take the next step?

Answer these through the page, not necessarily in five labeled sections.

### Headline and body rules

- Pair expressive headlines with concrete explanatory language.
- Make the offer understandable without requiring visitors to decode a clever line.
- Prefer meaningful outcomes and specifics over unsupported superlatives.
- Connect benefits to a credible mechanism, example, or proof.
- Put evidence near the claim it supports.
- Include relevant scope, constraints, process, timing, pricing factors, and exclusions when verified.
- Vary paragraph length naturally. Do not force rhythm through arbitrary fragments.
- Avoid repeating headings in closing sentences.
- Do not pad pages to hit an invented SEO word count.
- Use questions only when they reflect real customer questions and improve comprehension.
- Read the copy aloud for awkwardness, stiffness, and credibility.

### House language preferences

- No em dashes.
- No “It's not X, it's Y” or “No X, no Y, just Z” scaffolding.
- No reflexive strings such as “Fast. Simple. Proven.”
- No “your trusted partner,” “one-stop shop,” “look no further,” “we pride ourselves,” or “in today's fast-paced world.”
- No default CTA heading “Ready to get started?”
- No formulaic “Whether you're X or Y...” openings.
- No filler such as seamless, elevate, unlock, journey, empower, tailored, game-changer, delve, leverage, robust, cutting-edge, streamline, holistic, comprehensive, solutions, harness, or landscape when it substitutes for meaning.

Flag the listed words for contextual review. Do not corrupt a legitimate service name, factual explanation, or attributed quote merely to eliminate a word. Preserve testimonial meaning and do not silently rewrite quotations into invented language.

### The specificity test

Replace the business name with a realistic competitor's name. If the page still reads as an interchangeable pitch, strengthen it with relevant, verified details.

Not every sentence must be exclusive. Service labels and clear explanations can be shared across a category. The page as a whole must give the visitor a credible reason to choose.

### CTAs and microcopy

- Give each page one dominant conversion goal; repeat the CTA where useful.
- Allow supporting actions such as seeing projects or checking service coverage.
- Use labels that describe the next step: “Request a project estimate,” “See available appointments,” or “View completed kitchens,” when accurate.
- Avoid “Get Started” and “Learn More” as default buttons.
- Distinguish an inquiry from a confirmed booking or purchase.
- Explain the next step without inventing timing or commitments.
- Write input labels, help text, errors, confirmation messages, empty states, and booking handoffs with the same care as marketing copy.

## 8. UX and conversion

- Use familiar navigation labels and visible current-page states.
- Make the main offer, area served when relevant, and next action easy to find.
- Preserve ordinary scrolling and browser navigation.
- Do not hide critical information behind hover, complex animation, or unnecessary interactions.
- Give mobile users a complete experience, not a compressed desktop layout.
- Use sticky headers or CTAs only when they help; verify they do not obstruct content, focus, forms, or mobile keyboards.
- Avoid forced popups, artificial scarcity, and unnecessary interruptions.
- Ask only for form information needed at that stage.
- Preserve entered values after recoverable errors and provide useful recovery guidance.
- Show accurate success, loading, error, and empty states.
- Prevent accidental duplicate submissions where relevant.
- Make phone and email actions usable and verify their destinations.
- Complete external booking or payment handoffs and explain meaningful transitions.

### Conversion verification

A primary form must be tested through the configured destination, using an authorized test method. A green success message alone is insufficient. Record whether delivery was verified, simulated, or blocked by missing access.

Do not send real customer communications or incur charges merely to test a flow. Use approved test recipients or provider test modes. Flag the remaining production verification if those are unavailable.

Track successful outcomes separately from clicks. A phone-link click is not proof of a completed call; an external booking-link click is not proof of a booking.

## 9. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA using automated checks and manual inspection. Do not claim conformance solely because a scanner passes.

- Use semantic landmarks, meaningful headings, real links for navigation, and buttons for actions.
- Provide keyboard access, visible focus, and a logical focus order.
- Make menus and dialogs manageable with a keyboard, including focus return and dismissal where appropriate.
- Provide a skip link where useful for bypassing repeated navigation.
- Use sufficient text and interface contrast; do not rely on color alone.
- Give inputs persistent labels, helpful instructions, and programmatically associated errors.
- Ensure controls have appropriate accessible names and generous touch targets.
- Check zoom and narrow-width reflow without loss of content or functionality.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid flashing or disorienting effects.
- Caption meaningful spoken video and provide appropriate alternatives for media.
- Write informative image alternatives according to purpose and context.
- Use empty alt text for decorative images. Do not insert location keywords into every alt attribute.
- Provide text equivalents for meaningful diagrams and essential information.

Treat major keyboard, form, contrast, and content-access barriers as functional defects. Document limitations in embedded third-party tools rather than claiming control over inaccessible vendor components.

## 10. SEO architecture and on-page execution

### Search intent

- Assign each important indexable page a distinct intent and role.
- Understand the actual search results and customer need before choosing a page format.
- Use available first-party search data and reliable research. Do not invent keyword volume, difficulty, or rankings.
- Build useful service hubs and supporting pages with contextual links.
- Avoid multiple thin pages competing for the same need.
- Use terminology customers understand without awkward repetition.

### Page requirements

- A descriptive, page-specific title that accurately identifies the content.
- One clear main H1 by project convention, supported by logical heading structure. Do not claim that a specific H1 count alone determines rankings.
- A useful, distinct meta description for important indexable pages. Do not promise Google will use it verbatim.
- Concise, stable, descriptive URLs.
- Relevant original content and proof appropriate to the visitor's task.
- Crawlable internal links with descriptive anchor text.
- An intentional canonical URL reflecting the preferred version.
- Appropriate image dimensions, filenames, and alternatives without keyword stuffing.
- Accurate social-sharing metadata and usable share imagery.

Treat title and description length tools as preview aids, not rigid character-count ranking rules. Check how wording reads and what may truncate.

### Local SEO

- Maintain accurate business identity and contact information across authoritative profiles and the website. Formatting differences alone are not the central issue; avoid conflicting facts.
- Reflect the real public address or service-area model. Never invent an office or expose a private address merely to fill a template.
- Describe actual areas served and relevant service limitations.
- Build location pages only when useful local details justify them: genuine projects, service logistics, local process, staff, or verified practical differences.
- Do not mass-produce city-name substitutions or doorway pages.
- Use authentic reviews with accurate attribution and appropriate permissions.
- Keep hours, booking links, profile links, and service information current.
- Use map embeds when helpful, not as mandatory decoration.

### Structured data

- Select types appropriate to the actual entity and page.
- Ensure markup matches supported visible information and current feature requirements.
- Keep business identity consistent and avoid conflicting plugin-generated markup.
- Never fabricate ratings, reviews, prices, authors, events, or locations.
- Do not add review markup simply to pursue self-serving stars.
- Validate syntax and relevant eligibility separately. Valid markup does not guarantee a rich result.
- Add FAQs because they help visitors, not because every business is eligible for FAQ rich results.

### AI search visibility

- Make important facts available as accessible text.
- Explain services, entities, relationships, scope, and evidence clearly.
- Answer real questions directly when it improves the page.
- Maintain useful internal links and current business information.
- Use genuine expertise, original examples, and attributable claims.
- Review crawler access intentionally; distinguish search discovery from model-training controls.
- Do not promise citations or recommendations from an LLM.
- Do not treat `llms.txt`, hidden prompts, special AI schema, or other speculative tactics as required ranking infrastructure.

Google's published guidance states that AI Overviews and AI Mode use the established SEO foundations and require no special AI files or additional schema. This statement applies to Google's features; other systems may have their own access policies.

## 11. Technical SEO and migration

- Ensure important content and navigation can be crawled and rendered reliably.
- Prefer pre-rendered or server-rendered important marketing content where the platform makes it practical.
- Check robots directives, canonical tags, redirects, status codes, and sitemap contents in the actual environment.
- Include canonical, indexable, successful URLs in XML sitemaps; keep modification dates honest.
- Use real 404 responses for missing pages and a helpful custom 404 experience.
- Handle intentional removals and redirects deliberately.
- Avoid redirect loops, unnecessary chains, and blanket redirects of unrelated removed pages to the homepage.
- Verify HTTPS and the preferred host version.
- Protect previews appropriately. Robots.txt is not access control, and blocking crawling can prevent a crawler from seeing a noindex directive.
- Verify production does not inherit preview noindex settings.
- Verify Search Console ownership and sitemap submission when authorized and available.

### Required redesign inventory

Before changing an existing site, capture important URLs, titles, headings, content, conversions, backlinks or landing-page performance when available, and existing redirect rules.

Map changed URLs to their closest meaningful replacement. Preserve successful content and discoverability unless there is a documented reason to change them. Test important old URLs on the actual deployed environment after launch.

A redesigned site can be more attractive and still damage search traffic. URL continuity, content continuity, and measurement are part of the build.

## 12. Performance and engineering

### Performance targets

Target good Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile of real visits, assessed separately for mobile and desktop:

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Largest Contentful Paint | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint | 200 milliseconds or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift | 0.1 or less |

Use lab tests before launch, then field data when available. A new site may lack enough field data. Lighthouse does not establish real-user INP or prove a field pass. Do not trade useful functionality for a vanity score or promise rankings from a performance score.

### Implementation expectations

- Use the simplest maintainable architecture that meets the actual requirements.
- Reuse accessible behaviors and technical foundations, while designing client-specific composition and content.
- Avoid large dependencies for minor effects.
- Load third-party scripts intentionally and assess their cost.
- Optimize fonts and images; do not lazy-load the primary above-the-fold image by default.
- Reserve layout space for media and embeds.
- Keep navigation, reading, and primary actions available without waiting for decorative motion.
- Test supported browsers and representative mobile conditions.
- Keep source code and generated build output distinct.
- Document build, preview, deployment, environment-variable names, and rollback procedures.
- Never place credentials or private API keys in client-side code, public repositories, screenshots, or handoff documents.
- Use appropriate server-side validation, abuse controls, and trusted integrations for forms and transactions.
- Match privacy disclosures and consent behavior to the site's actual data handling and applicable requirements; seek qualified review when legal judgment is needed.
- Keep content editable at the level the client can realistically maintain.

### Platform constraints

For Squarespace, Craft, WordPress, hosted booking systems, or custom HTML blocks, inspect actual capabilities before promising changes. Scope styles to avoid collisions. Do not pretend global navigation or server behavior can be controlled from a restricted content block.

Do not migrate platforms, replace working integrations, or introduce paid dependencies without a clear task need and appropriate authorization.

## 13. Prevent repeated corrections and regressions

At the start of each work session, read the brief, approved direction, relevant code, and decision log. Identify the smallest coherent scope before editing.

- Preserve approved copy, layouts, assets, interactions, and SEO settings unless the task requires changing them.
- Do not rewrite a whole page to fix one section.
- Do not silently swap fonts, dependencies, image sources, or shared components.
- Record whether feedback applies globally, to this client, to one page, or to one element.
- Do not generalize one client's preference to every client.
- Prefer fixing the source of a layout problem over accumulating overrides.
- Do not refactor unrelated code during a focused visual or copy revision.
- If a shared component changes, inspect representative affected pages.
- Keep a stable checkpoint before substantial revisions.
- Compare the rendered result with the approved state at the same viewport.
- Confirm requested changes and check that adjacent content remains intact.

### Decision log format

| Date | Scope | Decision | Reason | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Record date | Global / client / page / component | Exact approved direction | Specific rationale or feedback | Approved / proposed / superseded |

Retain accepted and rejected examples with reasons. “Generic” is insufficient. “Opening never identifies the service and repeats the same three-card structure four times” is actionable.

## 14. Production workflow

### Stage 1: Establish the facts

Read source materials and existing instructions. Inventory the current site when applicable. Complete the brief and claims register. Identify only material gaps.

### Stage 2: Establish the direction

Recommend a specific concept, references, page architecture, content priorities, and design system. State how these choices fit the business.

### Stage 3: Prove the direction

Build a representative sample with realistic content and desktop/mobile views. Obtain direction approval unless already provided. Avoid expanding a weak concept across ten pages.

### Stage 4: Build systematically

Extend approved tokens, components, and patterns. Write page-specific content. Finish secondary pages, forms, metadata, media states, and integrations.

### Stage 5: Critique and verify

Review in distinct passes: creative quality, copy and conversion, usability and accessibility, technical SEO, functionality, performance, and regression. These are review perspectives and do not require multiple agents or tools.

Fix high-impact defects, rerun relevant checks, and stop optional testing once the remaining risk is understood. Do not endlessly polish inconsequential details while core tasks remain unfinished.

### Stage 6: Deliver a reviewable preview

Provide the preview, concise change summary, evidence of completed checks, unresolved issues, and decisions needed. Do not claim “everything passes” when checks were unavailable.

### Stage 7: Launch and verify

After explicit production approval, deploy the reviewed version. Verify the live site, key URLs, conversion path, redirects, indexing directives, and analytics. Retain rollback information.

## 15. QA gates and evidence

### Automated checks

Automate objective failures where practical:

- Build errors and applicable type/lint checks.
- Broken internal links and referenced assets.
- Placeholder content, accidental example domains, empty navigation destinations, and unsupported public promises.
- Missing or conflicting required metadata.
- Redirect loops and unintended response codes.
- Sitemap and canonical inconsistencies.
- Basic accessibility violations.
- Critical functional checks for primary conversions.

Search customer-facing content and relevant source files. Exclude dependency directories, generated bundles where inappropriate, internal documentation, and fixtures from copy-style scans. Review legitimate uses before declaring failures. A real `#section-id` link is not the same as a dead `href="#"`.

Potential review terms include eyebrow, kicker, overline, em dash, PLACEHOLDER, Lorem, TODO, example.com, Get Started, Learn More, backdrop-filter, background-clip: text, and the filler phrases in Section 7. CSS tokens and code terms are clues, not conclusive evidence of poor design.

### Visual checks

Inspect rendered pages, not only source code. Use full-page views and close inspection of key sections.

- Representative narrow phones, wider phones, tablet, and desktop.
- A useful starting set is 360, 390, 768, and 1440 CSS pixels wide, adapted to the audience and platform.
- Headline wrapping, reading width, image crops, spacing, alignment, and visual hierarchy.
- Navigation, footer, menu-open state, form errors, confirmations, and dialogs.
- Zoom/reflow, keyboard focus, reduced motion, and sticky-element overlap.
- Representative long content and real copy, not just short dummy text.
- No horizontal overflow, clipped content, broken media, or unreadable overlays.

### Functional and launch checks

- Every visible action performs its stated function.
- Primary conversion tested, with delivery status recorded.
- Booking and external destinations correct.
- Favicon, social image, and share metadata appropriate.
- No unresolved application errors affecting the experience; investigate console errors and document material third-party limitations.
- Production status codes, redirects, canonicals, robots directives, and sitemap verified.
- Tracking measures the intended events without treating every click as a conversion.
- No public placeholders, fake proof, or sensitive information.
- Source and deployment instructions available.
- Production launch approved.

### Review rubric

Use evidence rather than an invented numerical “elite score.”

| Dimension | Passing question |
| --- | --- |
| Distinctiveness | Does this feel like this specific business rather than a recolored template? |
| Clarity | Can an unfamiliar visitor understand the offer and next step quickly? |
| Craft | Are typography, imagery, spacing, alignment, and secondary states consistently resolved? |
| Credibility | Are important claims supported and accurately attributed? |
| Flow | Does the sequence help the visitor make a decision without repeated filler? |
| Usability | Can people complete the main task across devices and input methods? |
| Discoverability | Are relevant pages useful, crawlable, connected, and correctly configured? |
| Maintainability | Can the site be updated without fragile patches or unnecessary dependencies? |

The model's self-review is supporting evidence. Mike's review and observed user behavior remain necessary for evaluating taste and effectiveness.

### QA report format

| Check | Scope/environment | Result | Evidence | Remaining action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Pages, viewport, browser, or deployment | Pass / fail / not tested / not applicable | Observation or saved output | Fix, owner, or limitation |

Never turn “not tested” into “pass.” Fix launch blockers before requesting final approval. If a blocker requires missing access or a business decision, state the precise blocker and complete the other authorized work.

## 16. Measurement and ongoing SEO

Before launch, define the business outcome: qualified inquiries, completed bookings, purchases, applications, or another real result. Establish the measurement method and available baseline.

After launch, evaluate search visibility, landing-page traffic, conversion completion, lead quality, and operational follow-through. Interpret small samples cautiously. A traffic increase with poor-fit inquiries may be a poor outcome.

Separate the one-time build from continuing work:

- Business-profile updates and review acquisition.
- Accurate listings and business information.
- Relevant partnerships, earned mentions, and links.
- Original project stories and useful content updates.
- Search Console and conversion monitoring.
- Accessibility, dependency, and performance maintenance.

Assign an owner and cadence when ongoing work is in scope. Do not imply a one-time launch checklist performs these tasks automatically.

| Original idea | Treatment in this playbook |
| --- | --- |
| Every broken rule fails QA | Severity separated into blockers, house defaults, and contextual guidance |
| Ban visual and copy clichés | Retained, with positive art direction and customer-purpose requirements |
| One primary CTA | Clarified as one dominant goal, with repetition and useful supporting actions |
| Real photo above the fold | Authentic assets preferred; hero composition determined by quality and message |
| Full-width reviews with real names | Verified, authorized attribution and relevant placement; no fixed layout |
| Maximum two fonts | Retained as a default, expanded into a typography system |
| Vary every section | Composition varies inside a coherent visual system |
| Ban all filler words | Contextual review to protect legitimate terminology and quotations |
| Exact business-profile matching | Accurate consistent facts, appropriate public-address handling |
| Per-image SEO alt text | Purpose-based alternatives, including empty alt text for decorative images |
| Scan everything with grep | Targeted scans plus manual review, excluding irrelevant code and dependencies |
| Inspect people for AI artifacts | Source and rights tracking plus separate visual inspection |
| Basic local SEO signals | Full architecture, on-page, technical, migration, local, and AI-search guidance |
| Screenshot desktop and phone | Multi-width visual, interaction-state, accessibility, and regression checks |

## Appendix B: Reusable prompts

### New website kickoff

```text
Read louislynn-website-master-playbook.md and the supplied project materials before coding. Treat it as the project's operating standard, subject to the current task and governing environment instructions.

Build a distinctive website for this business with specific art direction, credible copy, clear visitor flow, accessible interactions, sound SEO, and maintainable implementation.

First establish the facts, audience, offer, proof, conversion goal, platform constraints, and asset quality. Complete the Project Record using existing sources. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the work; proceed on reasonable reversible assumptions elsewhere.

Recommend one clear creative direction with annotated references and a page map. Translate aesthetic adjectives into concrete choices. Give the site a memorable business-relevant idea. Avoid the house anti-patterns.

Build a representative desktop and mobile design using realistic copy and assets. Make the direction reviewable before extending it through the full site. If the direction is already approved, continue without asking again.

Maintain a claims register and decision log. Preserve approvals. Inspect rendered pages and test the primary conversion. Report actual QA evidence and anything untested.

Deliver a completed preview. Wait for explicit approval before pushing the reviewed version to production.
```

### Existing website redesign

```text
Read the master playbook, current project record, existing site, and repository instructions. Inventory important URLs, content, metadata, conversions, assets, integrations, redirects, and available performance evidence before changing the site.

Identify what already works and preserve it unless there is a supported reason to change it. Recommend the creative and structural improvements that will make the largest difference.

Develop the representative design, record approvals, and implement the redesign with a URL migration map and relevant regression checks. Do not substitute generic copy for verified business detail or discard useful existing content merely to simplify the layout.

Provide a tested preview, redirect plan, unresolved limitations, and rollback approach. Do not push to production without explicit approval.
```

### Focused revision

```text
Read the master playbook and approved project decisions. Implement the requested change using the smallest coherent scope.

Preserve approved copy, layout, assets, behavior, and SEO settings outside that scope. Do not redesign neighboring sections or introduce new dependencies unless necessary to solve the request.

Inspect the changed result at relevant desktop and mobile widths. Check shared components and adjacent content for regressions. Record any lasting preference with its correct scope.

Report what changed, what you actually checked, and any remaining limitation. Deliver the updated preview without pushing live unless this change has production authorization.
```

### Creative quality review

```text
Review the rendered site against the master playbook and approved creative direction. Do not judge quality by the absence of banned words alone.

Identify the most consequential weaknesses in distinctiveness, clarity, typography, image direction, hierarchy, page rhythm, proof, copy, mobile behavior, and conversion flow. Name the affected page or section, the observed problem, its consequence, and a concrete correction.

Distinguish factual or functional defects from aesthetic preferences. Prioritize the five changes with the greatest likely benefit. Do not invent user-testing results or conversion impact. If authorized to implement, fix those issues and verify the affected experience while preserving approved decisions.
```

### Prelaunch verification

```text
Verify the actual preview against the master playbook. Check primary conversions, link destinations, visual behavior, accessibility, metadata, crawlability, redirects, production configuration, and performance using available tools.

Return a concise evidence table with pass, fail, not tested, and not applicable states. Fix authorized launch blockers before presenting the result. State precisely where access or business confirmation prevents verification.

Do not claim compliance, rankings, successful delivery, or passed checks without evidence. Prepare the release for Mike's final approval, including the exact reviewed version and rollback method.
```
